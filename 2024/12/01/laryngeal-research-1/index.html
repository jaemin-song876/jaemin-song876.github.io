    Laryngeal Disease Classification Using Voice Data: Octave-band vs. Mel-frequency Filters

    Jaemin Song, Hyunbum Kim, Yong Oh Lee
    Heliyon, 2024

    • Frequencies below the 1st formant are crucial for detecting laryngeal diseases.
    • 1/3 octave band filter is suitable for analyzing below the 1st formant frequency.
    • Grad-CAM images confirmed the importance of frequencies below the 1st formant.
    • The 1/3 octave band filter is superior to MFCC in distinguishing laryngeal diseases.

    Why voice-based diagnosis for laryngeal disease?

    Consider a patient suffering from early-stage vocal dysfunction. Traditional diagnostic methods like endoscopy can be invasive and expensive. In contrast, voice signals contain rich information about vocal tract behavior and can be captured easily using a microphone.

    Voice-based classification offers a non-invasive, cost-effective approach that can aid in:

    • Early screening for laryngeal cancer
    • Monitoring dysphonia progression
    • Differentiating between healthy and pathological voice signals

    This raises fundamental questions in system design:

    • Which feature representation (octave vs. MFCC) yields better classification accuracy?
    • Can we detect early signs of vocal disorders using limited audio samples?
    • How do preprocessing steps (e.g., noise reduction, frame windowing) affect final predictions?

    Introducing our voice classification pipeline

    We design and compare two processing pipelines based on the type of spectrogram input:

    • Octave-band spectrograms: Emphasize energy distribution across frequency bands aligned with human auditory perception.
    • MFCCs: Popular features in speech recognition, but may discard subtle frequency dynamics useful for disease detection.


    We delve into the technical nuances of Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) and octave frequency spectrum energy (OFSE), comparing their suitability and effectiveness in processing voice data for the detection of laryngeal cancer

    Unlike MFCC, OFSE uses an octave-band filter, which better preserves low-frequency details—important for analyzing pathological voices.

    It processes audio by segmenting it into frames and converting them to the frequency domain, but instead of the mel scale, it applies a logarithmic octave filter bank.

    This enhances resolution in low frequencies, making OFSE especially effective for detecting subtle anomalies in laryngeal pathologies.

    Use Cases for this research

    Real-world use of this pipeline includes:

    • Remote health monitoring: Easily record and classify voice samples on smartphones.
    • Telemedicine applications: Enable scalable, non-invasive pre-screening.
    • AI-assisted diagnostics: Help clinicians prioritize patients for in-depth examination.
